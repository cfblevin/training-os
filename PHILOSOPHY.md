# Training OS — training philosophy

What the program is trying to do, why it is built this way, and what would make it change.
Written to be argued with. Nothing here is settled by authority; where evidence is thin it says so.

---

## 1. What we are actually building

Four goals, in your words, translated into training terms:

| You said | What that means in the plan |
| --- | --- |
| Grow my shoulders | Lateral deltoid volume, high frequency, 14–18 hard sets/week |
| Grow my lats | Vertical pulling as a foundation pattern, 14–18 sets/week, separated from upper-back volume in the ledger |
| Tighter, slimmer mid-waist | Body composition plus shoulder-to-waist ratio; bracing-style core work, no loaded lateral flexion |
| Bigger quads | Two quad exposures a week, set up for long femurs, 14–18 sets/week |

Your measurements make one thing unusually clear. Shoulders 19 in against hips 17 in is a 1.12 ratio.
Every inch you add to the delts and lats improves that ratio directly; the waist itself is mostly a
body-composition variable. So three of your four goals point at the same work, and the plan leans into that.

Your 18 in femur against a 34 in torso and 16.5 in tibia means long lower body on a short trunk, and a
+1.8 in ape index means long arms. Neither rules anything out. They change **setup**, not **allowed**.

## 2. What actually drives muscle growth

Ranked by how confident the evidence is, strongest first.

1. **Progressive overload on a stable movement.** More force over time through a usable range. This is
   the one non-negotiable, and it is the one thing that requires you to keep doing the *same* exercise
   long enough to measure it.
2. **Enough weekly hard sets.** Roughly 10–20 per muscle per week is the well-supported window. Growth
   increases with volume inside that range and the returns flatten after it while fatigue keeps climbing.
3. **Proximity to failure.** Sets need to be genuinely hard — about 0–3 reps left in the tank. Note the
   app does **not** ask you to log this. Rating effort every set is a tax with poor data quality; the rep
   target plus the benchmark does the same job.
4. **Frequency of 2+ sessions per muscle per week.** Mostly useful because it lets you spread volume out,
   not because the muscle needs the extra signal. Past 2–3×/week, equal volume gives roughly equal results.
5. **Consistency and recoverability.** The program you actually complete for six months beats the better
   program you abandon in three weeks.

**What does not drive growth, despite feeling like it does:** soreness. DOMS tracks novelty and eccentric
stress, not hypertrophy. A brutal first session on a new movement is sore because it is new. That is worth
saying plainly, because it bears directly on your question below.

## 3. Your "odd pairings" — the honest answer

You paired upper back + biceps, upper back + triceps, upper chest + biceps, and found it worked well:
more soreness, more muscles stimulated.

**What was probably real:** those pairings fix a genuine problem with a classic push/pull/legs day. In
a 20-set PPL session the last exercises are performed on a badly fatigued muscle. Pairing one large
pattern with a muscle that pattern left fresh means the small muscle gets *quality* sets, not leftover
sets. It also spreads weekly volume over more days, which is the main practical benefit of higher
frequency. Those are real mechanisms and they are why this plan is built the same way.

**What was probably not real:** the soreness itself as evidence. Changing exercises produces soreness
whether or not it produces growth. If we chase soreness we will keep changing movements, and changing
movements is exactly what destroys your ability to see progressive overload.

**What the evidence says about pairing specifically:** very little, directly. Agonist–antagonist pairing
(pull then push) can preserve performance in the second exercise and saves time. Beyond that, *which*
muscles share a session matters far less than total weekly volume per muscle, effort, and overload.
So the pairings are chosen for **freshness and logistics**, not because some pairing is anabolically
special. I am not going to sell you that it is.

**What about variety and boredom:** this is where I will not pretend. Some evidence suggests varying
exercises can help regional growth within a muscle and clearly helps adherence. Other evidence shows
that switching too often costs you the load progression that drives growth in the first place.

So the app splits the session in two, explicitly:

- **Anchor lifts — three per working day, locked for the whole block.** These are the only lifts whose
  numbers we take seriously. They carry the benchmark, the load suggestions and the block review.
- **Rotating slots — everything else.** Each one cycles through a pool, one movement per week, so the
  same variation never lands twice running. The app labels these in the workout: *"Rotating slot — this
  movement changes next week. Work the rep range; the load is not the point here."*

That gets you both things honestly. Roughly 59% of your weekly sets on working days sit on tracked
anchors, and the remaining 41% is free to be different every week. Boredom lives in the accessory work;
progression lives in the anchors. Neither has to compromise for the other.

## 4. The split

Seven training days, because you train seven days. The load is deliberately uneven: six working days
and one genuinely light day, rather than seven medium days. Frequency is not the same thing as fatigue,
and recovery has to come from somewhere.

Each day leads with one compound that drives progression, then adds work for a region that compound
left fresh.

| Day | Session | Anchors (locked for the block) | Rotating |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Lats & Biceps | Lat pulldown · One-arm DB row · EZ-bar curl | Straight-arm, rear delt, lateral raise, core |
| Tue | Upper Chest & Delts | Incline barbell bench · Machine chest press · DB lateral raise | Fly, triceps, rear delt |
| Wed | Quads & Core | Machine hack squat · DB Bulgarian split squat · Leg extension | Leg curl, calves, core |
| Thu | Upper Back & Triceps | Barbell row · Neutral-grip pulldown · EZ-bar skull crusher | Face pull, pressdown, lateral raise, second row |
| Fri | Delts & Upper Chest | Machine shoulder press · Incline DB press · Cable lateral raise | Fly, biceps, core |
| Sat | Hinge & Legs | Trap-bar deadlift · Leg press · Lying leg curl | Back extension, calves, core |
| Sun | Priority & Conditioning | *none — everything rotates* | Lateral delts, lats, upper chest, core, and your run or swim |

Why this and not push/pull/legs:

- **No day repeats the pattern of the day before.** Vertical pull Monday, incline press Tuesday, squat
  Wednesday. Consecutive training days are always a different pattern, which is what makes seven days
  survivable.
- **Lateral delts appear five days a week**, at 3–4 sets each time. They are small, recover fast and
  tolerate frequency — and they are your highest-leverage region for the shoulder-to-waist ratio.
- **Lats and upper back are separate days**, because you treat them as separate goals and the ledger
  counts them separately.
- **Quads get two very different exposures** (squat pattern Wednesday, leg press and unilateral Saturday)
  rather than one enormous leg day.
- **Sunday is real recovery.** 11 low-load sets, nothing heavy, no anchors, and the natural home for a
  hard run.
- **One note on lower-back traffic.** Barbell rows on Thursday and trap-bar deadlifts on Saturday are the
  two lifts that tax the lower back, and they sit two days apart with Friday's delts-and-chest session in
  between. That is deliberate. If Saturday's trap bar ever feels compromised by Thursday's rowing, the
  first thing to cut is a set of barbell rows, not a set of trap bar.

## 5. The anchor lifts and why each one

An anchor stays put for the whole block so that load progression means something. You picked these.

| Pattern | Your anchor | Why it holds up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | **Machine hack squat** | You said you don't like barbell squatting, and your build backs that up: an 18 in femur pushes the torso forward in any free squat. The hack squat supports the back, so femur length stops dictating the setup and the quads become the limiting factor. No barbell squat variant appears anywhere in the physique plan. |
| Unilateral | **DB Bulgarian split squat** | Each leg sets its own stance and the torso stays upright. Brutal but the best long-femur quad movement you have. |
| Hinge | **Trap-bar deadlift** | High handles and a more upright torso suit a short trunk on long legs. Heavier systemic cost than an RDL, which is why it sits on Saturday with a light Sunday behind it. |
| Vertical pull | **Lat pulldown** | 10 lb stack steps. You cannot progress a lift you can only load in big jumps, and at +1.8 in ape index the range is long and lat-dominant. Pull-ups stay available as a swap. |
| Horizontal pull | **Barbell row** | The most loadable row and the best raw upper-back stimulus. It costs lower-back freshness, so it runs at 4 sets of 6–10 rather than high reps, and Thursday also carries a rotating chest-supported row to add upper-back volume without more spinal load. |
| Incline press | **Incline barbell bench** | 2.5 lb plates mean 5 lb jumps, so progression stays visible for months. With long arms the range is long — 25–30° and a controlled bar path matter more for you than for most. |
| Flat press | **Machine chest press** | Stable pressing volume with no spotter and no stabilization tax, which is what you want from the second press of the day. |
| Overhead | **Machine shoulder press** | You already carry real axial load from the trap bar and barbell row. This adds shoulder work without stacking a third heavy spinal day, and front delts aren't a priority region anyway. |
| Lateral raise | **DB lateral raise (Tue) + cable lateral raise (Fri)** | Two tracked delt lifts, because lateral delts are your single highest-leverage region. |
| Knee extension | **Leg extension** | Direct quad work that loads in small steps — the cleanest progression signal in the whole lower body. |
| Knee flexion | **Lying leg curl** | Hamstring volume that doesn't compete with the trap bar for lower-back recovery. |
| Elbow flexion | **EZ-bar curl** | Wrist-friendly and loadable in 5 lb steps. |
| Elbow extension | **EZ-bar skull crusher** | The most loadable triceps movement; the long head gets stretched. |

Deliberately not in the library: assisted pull-up, machine pullover, pendulum squat, belt squat, weighted
side bends, loaded twists.

**What rotates.** Straight-arm pulldowns, rear-delt work, the third and fourth lateral-raise exposures,
flies, pressdowns and overhead extensions, calves, leg curls, hip extension and all core work. Each pool
holds two or three movements and advances one per week. You can widen or trim any pool in
Settings → Rotating slots, and swapping a single day's exercise never touches the plan.

## 6. Volume targets

Your chosen band: **14–18 hard sets per week for priority regions, 10–12 for the rest.**

The ledger counts **regions**, not body parts, because "Shoulders: 31 sets" tells you nothing when what
you care about is lateral delts. Lateral delts, front delts and rear delts are counted separately; lats
apart from upper back; upper chest apart from the rest of the chest.

A set counts **1.0** toward the region it trains directly and **0.5** toward each region it trains
meaningfully but not primarily. A lat pulldown is 1.0 to Lats and 0.5 to Biceps and Upper back. This is
why the arm bands are set higher (12–18): arms collect a lot of half-credit from the pulls and presses,
and pretending otherwise would have you adding direct arm work you do not need.

## 7. Progression rules

**Double progression, benchmark-driven.** Every loaded exercise has a set count and a rep range.

1. Work within the rep range at the current load.
2. Add reps session to session until **every planned set** reaches the **top of the range**.
3. That is the benchmark. The app then offers a load increase — `Suggestion: 125 lb next time` —
   with **Accept** or **Keep current**. It never changes a load on its own.
4. On a load increase you will drop back toward the bottom of the range. That is the cycle working.

Details that matter:

- **Unilateral movements are judged on the weaker side.** Left and right are logged separately, and the
  benchmark only counts when the weaker side hits the top of the range.
- **No effort ratings.** No RIR, no RPE, no readiness score. The instruction "take the set close to
  failure" lives in your head; the app measures load and reps, which are objective.
- **Baselines are set once.** Starting loads are estimates until you confirm them; after that,
  progression comes only from what you log.
- **Bodyweight movements with no increment never generate a suggestion.** You progress those with reps.
- **When a lift stalls** (three sessions with no rep improvement): the honest order of operations is
  (1) check sleep and food, (2) drop the load ~10% and rebuild, (3) if it stalls again on the same
  movement, change the anchor at the next block review. Adding more sets is the *last* lever, not the first.
- **Rotating slots are not held to this.** They still record load and reps, and they will still offer a
  suggestion if you hit the top of the range — but the app does not show a benchmark checkpoint on them,
  because chasing load on a movement that changes next week is a category error.

### The block

Anchors are locked for **6 weeks**. At the end the app runs a review and tells you, per anchor, what the
first logged top set was, what the most recent one was, and one of four verdicts:

| Verdict | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Progressing** | Load or reps went up across the block | Nothing. It is working. |
| **Holding** | Same numbers start to finish | Usually load or recovery, not the exercise. Deload ~10% and rebuild before you change anything. |
| **Needs review** | Numbers went backwards | Check recovery first; if that is fine, swap the anchor. |
| **Too early / no data** | Fewer than two logged sessions | Log it before judging it. |

Then you start the next block and the anchors lock again. Change anchors at a review, not mid-block —
mid-block changes are exactly how you end up unable to tell whether anything is working. The one
exception is pain: if a lift hurts, swap it the same day.

## 8. The waist question, answered honestly

You cannot reduce fat from one area by training it. There is no training variable that slims a waist.

What actually changes how the midsection looks:

1. **Body composition.** Nutrition. Not a training variable, and the app deliberately does not track it.
2. **Shoulder-to-waist ratio.** At 19 in shoulders and 17 in hips, widening the top is the fastest visible
   change available to you — which is already three of your five priorities.
3. **Not thickening the waist.** Heavy loaded lateral flexion and weighted rotation can add oblique girth.
   None of it is programmed here, and none of it is in the library. Core work is anti-extension (ab wheel,
   plank, Pallof) and controlled flexion (cable crunch, leg raise) — bracing quality without girth.

## 9. Running and swimming

They are logged, never programmed. They do not appear in the split, they do not count as hard sets, and
they have their own line in History.

Two practical notes, since you are ramping both up:

- **Interference is real but modest, and it is concentrated in the legs.** High-volume or high-intensity
  running competes with lower-body strength and size adaptation, most of all when it lands close to a
  hard leg session. Swimming is low-impact and interferes far less.
- **Where to put hard running:** Sunday (the light day), Monday or Thursday — all upper-body or rest-heavy
  days. Keep hard running away from Wednesday and Saturday, which carry your quad and hinge work. Easy
  running any day is fine.

If running becomes a real goal rather than conditioning, that is a programming change and we should talk
about it before the split absorbs it.

## 10. What would make me change this plan

- A priority region sits under target for three consecutive weeks → add a slot, do not add a whole day.
- The same anchor holds for a full block after a deload → swap it at the block review.
- Sessions consistently run past ~60 minutes → cut sets from non-priority regions first.
- Sleep or appetite degrade for more than a week → the light day becomes two light days before anything
  else changes.
- You get bored → the rotating slots already change weekly, and you can widen any pool. Leave the 18
  anchors alone until the block review unless something hurts, because those are the only things telling
  us whether any of this is working.
